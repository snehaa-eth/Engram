# Agent Long-Term Memory Management: State of the Art (2025 – Nov 2026)

Scope: how memory is written, consolidated, forgotten, and retrieved; RL-trained memory managers; memory OSes; sleep-time consolidation; graph and temporal memory; parametric vs external memory; and benchmark numbers with cost and latency. Research date: 2026-11-08. Labels used below: **[self-reported]** = numbers published by the vendor or authors themselves; **[secondary]** = taken from an aggregator or blog, not the primary paper; **[unverified]** = not confirmed against a primary source in this session.

---

## Q1. What new methods beat Mem0/Zep/Letta/full-context on LongMemEval or LoCoMo in 2025–2026, by how much, and at what token/latency cost?

### Takeaway
In 2026, headline scores on LoCoMo and LongMemEval-S cluster at 90–95%. Examples are Mem0's April 2026 algorithm (92.5 LoCoMo / 94.4 LME), Hindsight (91.4 LME / 89.6 LoCoMo), Mastra (~94.9 LME), EverMemOS (~92 LoCoMo) and Zep 2026 (94.7 LoCoMo with gpt-5.4). Almost all of these are self-reported, under incompatible judges, answer models and prompts. Treat the benchmarks as saturated and gameable. A strong full-context or naive-RAG baseline with a modern model often lands within 5–15 points. The clearest gains with controlled baselines appear in academic papers using the same backbone: LightMem, Hindsight and Memory-R1. These papers show both accuracy gains and large token reductions.

### Cited Findings

**Vendor systems (2026)**
- Mem0 "token-efficient memory algorithm" (April 2026, page updated Sep 2026). LoCoMo went from 71.4 to 92.5 overall (temporal 63.2→92.5, multi-hop 70.2→95.4). LongMemEval went from 67.8 to 94.4 overall (temporal reasoning 51.1→97.0, single-session-assistant 46.4→98.2, knowledge-update 79.5→93.6, multi-session 70.7→88.0). BEAM scored 64.1 at 1M and 48.6 at 10M. Mean tokens per query were 6,956 on LoCoMo, 6,787 on LME and ~6.7–6.9K on BEAM, against "25,000+" for full-context. Mem0 says extraction latency was "roughly halved". The setup is single-pass retrieval with no agentic loop, on the managed platform with "proprietary optimizations absent from the open-source SDK". Judge and answer models are not stated. Scores are ±1 point due to judge inconsistency. **[self-reported]** — [Mem0 blog](https://mem0.ai/blog/mem0-the-token-efficient-memory-algorithm)
- Mem0's own 2026 roundup lists other systems: Zep LoCoMo 80.32% at 189 ms (up to ~83% depending on config), Zep LongMemEval 71.2 (GPT-4o), Letta LoCoMo 74.0 (gpt-4o-mini, filesystem storage, no LongMemEval published), and OpenAI Memory LoCoMo 52.9 (carried over from Mem0's older content). **[self-reported/secondary; Mem0 is a competitor]** — [Mem0 State of AI Agent Memory 2026](https://mem0.ai/blog/state-of-ai-agent-memory-2026)
- A Korean blog aggregating a CoreDotToday audit (Oct 2026) collects score spreads. LoCoMo: Zep 94.7 (Zep 2026, gpt-5.4 with reasoning as both answerer and grader), 75.14 (Zep 2025, 10-run average, corrected from ~84%), 58.44 (Mem0's re-measurement), and 79.09 / 81.06 / 85.22 (as measured by MIRIX / EverMemOS). Mem0 92.5 (gpt-5 answering and grading, new grading prompt). EverMemOS 92.3. Backboard 90. Letta filesystem 74.0. Full-context gpt-4o-mini 74.0, and full-context gemini-3.1-flash-lite 80.7. A "bare hands" run with no conversation scored 25.9. LongMemEval: Mastra 94.87 (gpt-5-mini, unweighted per-type average; 93.6% with equal weights; 84.8% with gpt-4o), Zep 90.2 (scoreboard) vs 71.2 (Zep paper, full-context gpt-4o baseline 60.2), Supermemory "97%" (this is Recall@20, not QA accuracy), Google Vertex Memory Bank ~70 (original) vs 42.4 (Redis re-run, Sept 2026), full-context gemini-3.1-flash-lite 81.6. **[secondary; cites primary sources]** — [braindetox summary](https://braindetox.kr/en/posts/memory_benchmark_scores_reliability_2026.html), citing [CoreDotToday](https://core.today/blog/agent-memory-benchmarks-locomo-longmemeval-2026), [Penfield LoCoMo audit](https://dev.to/penfieldlabs/we-audited-locomo-64-of-the-answer-key-is-wrong-and-the-judge-accepts-up-to-63-of-intentionally-33lg), [Mem0 "How to read memory benchmarks"](https://mem0.ai/library/agent-memory/how-to-read-memory-benchmarks), and [Zep "Lies, Damn Lies & Statistics"](https://blog.getzep.com/lies-damn-lies-statistics-is-mem0-really-sota-in-agent-memory/)
- EverMemOS reports 83.00% on LongMemEval on its blog, while a comparison table lists 82%. ByteRover claims 92.8% on LME-S. **[self-reported, inconsistent]** — [Mem0 benchmarks library](https://mem0.ai/library/agent-memory/ai-memory-benchmarks-in-2026); [EverMind blog](https://evermind.ai/blogs/top-ai-memory-systems-benchmarked-in-2026)

**Academic methods with controlled baselines**
- **Hindsight** (Dec 2025, arXiv 2512.12818). Memory is split into four networks: world facts, agent experiences, synthesized entity summaries, and evolving beliefs. Operations are retain / recall / reflect, over a temporal, entity-aware layer. With an open-source 20B backbone, accuracy rose from 39% (full-context, same backbone) to 83.6%, and Hindsight also beats full-context GPT-4o. With a scaled backbone (Gemini-3 Pro, per a secondary source) it reaches 91.4% on LongMemEval and 89.61% on LoCoMo, against a prior best open system at 75.78% on LoCoMo. **[self-reported by authors]** — [arXiv 2512.12818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12818)
- **LightMem** (ICLR 2026, arXiv 2510.18866). It uses three stages inspired by Atkinson–Shiffrin: (1) LLMLingua-2 pre-compression and topic segmentation; (2) a topic-aware short-term buffer summarized into long-term entries; (3) long-term memory with soft inserts at test time and an offline "sleep-time" update queue processed in parallel. On LongMemEval-S with GPT-4o-mini, it scored 68.64 vs FullText 56.80, NaiveRAG 61.00, A-Mem 62.60, Mem0 53.61, MemoryOS 44.80 and LangMem 37.20. With Qwen3-30B-A3B it scored 70.20 vs A-Mem 65.20 and FullText 54.80. With GLM-4.6 it scored 73.20, tied with NaiveRAG. On LoCoMo with GPT-4o-mini it scored 72.99 vs FullText 71.83 and Mem0 61.69. With Qwen3-30B, FullText 74.87 beat LightMem 72.60. Efficiency: up to 38× fewer total tokens on LME (20.9× on LoCoMo) and up to 30× / 55.5× fewer API calls. Online-only costs were up to ~106–117× fewer tokens and 159–310× fewer API calls. — [arXiv 2510.18866v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18866v4)
- **Memory-R1** (ACL 2026; details in Q2). On LoCoMo with LLaMA-3.1-8B, LLM-judge J was 62.74 vs Mem0 45.68, MemoryOS 48.20 and A-Mem 44.76. Zero-shot on LongMemEval with Qwen-2.5-7B, J was 57.80 vs Mem0 46.80 and A-Mem 54.80. — [arXiv 2508.19828v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19828v5)
- **MemOS** (arXiv 2507.03724; v4 Dec 2025). It claims MemOS-1031 ranks first in all LoCoMo LLM-judge categories over MIRIX, Mem0, Zep, Memobase, MemU and Supermemory. An earlier MemOS-0630 configuration beat mem0, LangMem, Zep and OpenAI-Memory. Exact numbers were not extracted in this session. **[self-reported]** — [arXiv 2507.03724v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.03724v4)
- **BEAM / LIGHT** (ICLR 2026, arXiv 2510.27246). BEAM has 100 conversations and 2,000 validated questions, ranging from 100K to 10M tokens, covering 10 memory abilities. LIGHT combines episodic retrieval, working memory and a scratchpad. It improves 3.5%–12.69% on average over strong baselines, and the gains grow with length. The exception is Gemini-2.0-flash at 10M, where LIGHT slightly trails RAG. Against ReadAgent: ~0.311 vs 0.206 at 100K and ~0.309 vs 0.186 at 1M. — [arXiv 2510.27246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.27246); [ICLR 2026 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10006595)

**New, harder benchmarks**
- **LongMemEval-V2** (ICML 2026) uses 451 curated questions over multimodal web-agent trajectories (shopping, forum, admin and ServiceNow-style environments), with histories of 25M–115M tokens. Frontier LLMs score at most 14.1% without trajectory evidence. The best system is **AgentRunbook-C**, a scaffolded coding agent that greps and reads trajectory files, at 74.9% on Small and 70.1% on Medium. Scores are not comparable to LME-S. — [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/67423)
- On BEAM 10M, even Mem0's 2026 system collapses on temporal reasoning (61.8→16.3), event ordering (53.6→20.2) and multi-session reasoning (65.2→26.1). Contradiction resolution is only 35.7 at 1M. **[self-reported]** — [Mem0 blog](https://mem0.ai/blog/mem0-the-token-efficient-memory-algorithm)

**Benchmark reliability (critical for interpreting all of the above)**
- **Judge model.** Under the same Mem0-2025 prompt, the gpt-4o-mini judge gave 74.0%, Gemini 70.3% and Haiku 62.1%.
- **Judge prompt.** The same 1,540 answers scored 55.8% under LongMemEval-style grading and 81.4% under Mem0-2026 grading. Under Mem0-2026 grading, vague wrong answers passed 45.4% of the time. Penfield found the judge accepted up to 62.81% of intentionally wrong answers. **[secondary]** — [braindetox](https://braindetox.kr/en/posts/memory_benchmark_scores_reliability_2026.html); [Penfield audit](https://dev.to/penfieldlabs/we-audited-locomo-64-of-the-answer-key-is-wrong-and-the-judge-accepts-up-to-63-of-intentionally-33lg)
- **Answer-key errors.** LoCoMo has 6.4–7.0% wrong or ambiguous references (30.2% in open-domain), for an estimated ceiling of ~93.6%. The adversarial category (446 questions) is usually dropped. LongMemEval's file was swapped for a cleaned version in Sept 2025, changing 454 of 500 haystacks, so pre- and post-swap scores are not comparable. The 95% CI is about ±3 points, and the minimum detectable difference is ~4.5 points. **[secondary]** — same sources
- No source found independently reproduces the vendor systems under a common harness. Zep's 71.2% on LME appears in Zep's own arXiv paper (2501.13956). — [atlan](https://atlan.com/know/mem0-alternatives/); [Mem0 benchmarks library](https://mem0.ai/library/agent-memory/ai-memory-benchmarks-in-2026)

### Inferences
- Once you control for the answer and judge model, the gap between "memory system" and "full context with a strong long-context model" on LoCoMo and LME-S is often <10–15 points. The real differentiators are **cost** (~7K vs 25K+ tokens per query; LightMem's 20–100× token reductions) and **latency**. Top-line accuracy is not the main differentiator.
- Discriminative evaluation is moving to BEAM (1M–10M tokens), LongMemEval-V2 (25M–115M agent trajectories) and MemoryAgentBench. Temporal, ordering and contradiction abilities remain weak there.
- An "agentic file-search over raw logs" approach (AgentRunbook-C, Letta filesystem) is competitive with, or better than, curated memory stores on agent-experience tasks.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm full exact MemOS, MIRIX, Nemori, MemoryOS or EverMemOS numbers from primary papers in this session. Nemori, MIRIX and ReMemR1 numbers are **[unverified]**.
- I found no independent, common-harness 2026 leaderboard. A Redis re-run (Sept 2026) is mentioned only secondhand.
- Latency is rarely reported. Exceptions are Zep's 189 ms retrieval (secondary) and Memory-R1's appendix.

## Q2. Is "reconcile ADD/UPDATE/DELETE/NOOP with an LLM" still state of the art, or superseded by learned (RL) memory operations?

### Takeaway
Neither alone. The leading production system, Mem0, **abandoned LLM reconciliation in April 2026** in favor of **ADD-only append with temporal context, plus multi-signal retrieval**. Its stated reason is that UPDATE and DELETE destroyed context and were slow. LightMem similarly uses soft inserts online, with consolidation deferred offline. RL-trained managers (Memory-R1, Mem-α, MEM1, MemAgent) clearly beat prompted operations at the **same small-model size**. However, they are research-grade (7B–14B open models, small training sets) and are not shown to beat frontier-API pipelines. The 2026 consensus is: append raw, timestamped facts; consolidate asynchronously; and push "which fact is current" reasoning to retrieval and answer time.

### Cited Findings
- **Mem0's 2026 change.** The old pipeline used two LLM calls: extract, then ADD/UPDATE/DELETE against existing memories. The new pipeline is a single call that is ADD-only. Changed facts are stored alongside old ones "with temporal context", and memory count grows "by design: retrieval handles relevance ranking". Agent-generated facts (e.g. "I've booked your flight") are now first-class memories. Retrieval fuses semantic, keyword (BM25-like) and entity-match scores in parallel. Entity linking is built in through an `{collection}_entities` collection, and the graph `relations` field was removed. The stated rationale is that overwrites erased details and deletes removed info needed later. Gains claimed are temporal +29.6 and multi-hop +23.1 points. **[self-reported]** — [Mem0 token-efficient algorithm](https://mem0.ai/blog/mem0-the-token-efficient-memory-algorithm); [Mem0 State of Memory 2026](https://mem0.ai/blog/state-of-ai-agent-memory-2026); [Mem0 v2→v3 migration](https://docs.mem0.ai/migration/oss-v2-to-v3)
- **Memory-R1** (arXiv 2508.19828, ACL 2026) trains two agents. A Memory Manager picks ADD/UPDATE/DELETE/NOOP, and an Answer Agent distills ~60 retrieved memories and then answers. Training uses PPO/GRPO with outcome-only rewards, on just **152 LoCoMo QA pairs** (split 152/81/1,307). LoCoMo results with LLaMA-3.1-8B, as F1/B1/J: GRPO 45.02/37.51/62.74; Mem0 30.41/22.22/45.68; MemoryOS 35.04/27.99/48.20; and **Memory-SFT** (behavior cloning from GPT-5 trajectories) 42.81/32.98/58.76. The RL gain over strong SFT distillation is therefore ~4 J points. On Qwen-2.5 3B/7B/14B, RL improves over base, but at 7B the base J of 58.38 ≈ GRPO's 57.46, so the gain is mostly in F1. Latency: Answer Agent GRPO p50/p95 is 0.34/0.67 s, vs base at 0.65/3.07 s. Memory Manager p50 is ~2 s, and memory search p50 is <0.35 s. Memory distillation adds +4 F1 for GRPO. The motivating case is that a vanilla LLM does DELETE+ADD on "adopted a second dog", while the trained manager does an UPDATE. — [arXiv 2508.19828v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19828v5); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.583.pdf)
- **Mem-α** (arXiv 2509.25911) uses RL (Qwen3-4B) to learn memory construction over a core / episodic / semantic memory with tools. On validation it averages 0.642, vs long-context 0.588, RAG-Top2 0.567, MemAgent 0.236 and MEM1 0.111. On MemoryAgentBench (OOD) it scores 0.592, vs MemAgent 0.198 and MEM1 0.071. A base Qwen3-4B with the same tools scores 0.389, and gpt-4.1-mini with the same tools is also outperformed. Trained on ≤30K tokens, it generalizes to >400K (13×). The authors attribute MEM1's and MemAgent's weakness to flat single-paragraph memory. — [arXiv 2509.25911](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25911v1)
- **MEM1** (arXiv 2506.15841) does RL-learned constant-size internal-state consolidation for long multi-turn tasks. MEM1-7B improves performance 3.5× and cuts memory use 3.7× vs Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct on a 16-objective multi-hop QA task. — [secondary via search summary of Mem-α / MEM1](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/mem-learning-memory-construction-via-reinforcement-learning) **[secondary]**
- **Fine-Mem** (arXiv 2601.08435), a follow-up, reports +4.4% on the Memalpha benchmark and +7.2% on MemoryAgentBench over seven baselines. **[self-reported, unverified in detail]** — [arXiv 2601.08435](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08435)
- **LightMem** inserts without modification at test time ("soft updates"). Similar newer entries are queued per memory and reconciled offline in parallel during "sleep time". — [arXiv 2510.18866v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18866v4)
- **Zep/Graphiti** (bi-temporal knowledge graph) handles change by **invalidation with validity intervals**: old edges get an end time and are not deleted. This is closer to append-with-time than to destructive UPDATE/DELETE. **[secondary]** — [lhl/agentic-memory survey repo](https://github.com/lhl/agentic-memory)
- **Hindsight** keeps "evolving beliefs" as a separate network that the reflect operation updates in a "traceable way", alongside raw world facts and experiences. — [arXiv 2512.12818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12818)

### Inferences
- The field is converging on **non-destructive writes**: append with timestamps and provenance, as in Mem0 2026, Graphiti invalidation, LightMem soft inserts and Hindsight belief versioning. Destructive LLM DELETE/UPDATE at write time is now seen as a source of error.
- RL memory managers prove that memory operations are learnable and that small models can beat prompted Mem0-style pipelines. But (a) the comparisons use weak 7–8B backbones where every baseline scores low (J ≈ 45–62), (b) SFT distillation from a frontier model gets most of the gain, and (c) they require hosting your own fine-tuned model. For an API-model product, RL-trained managers are not yet a must-have. Distilling a small, cheap extraction model from frontier traces is a more practical middle path.
- The RL work's most transferable idea is the **answer-side "memory distillation" step**: have the answering model first select relevant memories from a large retrieved set, then answer. It is cheap, works with API models, and gives +4 F1 in ablations.

### Gaps
- MemAgent's (arXiv 2507.02259) and ReMemR1's own headline numbers (RULER/HotpotQA extrapolation to 3.5M tokens) were not verified in this session. **[unverified]**
- No source compares RL managers directly against Mem0-2026 ADD-only or frontier-model pipelines.

## Q3. Is parametric memory (Titans, memory layers, test-time training) practical yet for a product built on API models?

### Takeaway
No. Parametric memory is an active, promising research line: Titans, nested learning/HOPE, TNT, end-to-end TTT, and Meta's sparse memory finetuning. But it requires owning the model weights and serving infrastructure. No production deployment reports were found. For a product built on closed API models, memory must stay external (text, vectors, graph, files). The one practical "parametric-adjacent" option is periodic fine-tuning of an owned open model, and that is still research-grade for per-user memory.

### Cited Findings
- **Titans** (Google, 2025) adds a neural long-term memory module updated at inference through gradient steps, driven by surprise, momentum and gating. — [Titans overview](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/titans-learning-at-test-time) **[secondary]**
- **Nested Learning / HOPE** (Google, NeurIPS 2025) treats a model as nested optimization levels, where only the memory level updates at inference. **[secondary explainer]** — [Nested Learning & HOPE](https://budecosystem.alwaysdata.net/?p=1573)
- **TNT** (ICLR 2026) trains Titans and TTT models up to 17× faster than the most accurate baseline configuration. This makes the approach more trainable, but it is still about pretraining architectures. — [ICLR 2026 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/bdb30687f1c2255c29b11b0b45204ebe-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **Titan-Llama** retrofits attention LLMs with a hierarchical test-time-updated memory through post-training. It is the closest thing to deployment, but it is a single preprint. NVIDIA's "End-to-End Test-Time Training for Long Context" (Dec 2025) is related work. **[secondary]** — [search summary / emergentmind](https://api.emergentmind.com/topics/titans-and-ttt-models)
- **Sparse Memory Finetuning** (Meta FAIR + Berkeley, arXiv 2510.15103) updates only the memory-layer slots that are highly accessed on new data relative to pretraining. Learning a stream of TriviaQA facts, held-out NaturalQuestions F1 dropped **89% with full finetuning, 71% with LoRA, and only 11%** with sparse memory finetuning, at equal new-knowledge acquisition. It is evaluated only on fact and document streams, and is an unreplicated v1 preprint. — [arXiv 2510.15103](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15103v1)
- No production deployment reports were found for Titans or TTT-style memory. — search across [Titans/TTT sources](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/titans-learning-at-test-time)

### Inferences
- With API models (Claude, GPT, Gemini), you cannot update weights per user, so parametric memory is off the table except through vendor-provided features.
- Continual learning (sparse memory layers) could matter in 2027+ for self-hosted models. The architecture to watch is "memory layers + sparse slot updates", because it addresses catastrophic forgetting. Even so, per-user parametric memory raises unsolved deletion (GDPR "forget me") and auditability problems that external memory avoids.

### Gaps
- No quantified latency or cost for test-time-training memory in serving.
- No head-to-head of parametric vs external memory on LoCoMo, LME or BEAM was found.

## Q4. What should a 2026 memory product adopt? (Synthesis, consolidation/sleep, graph/temporal memory, forgetting)

### Takeaway
Recommended 2026 design, built from the primary evidence above:
1. **Write path**: cheap single-pass, ADD-only extraction of atomic facts, including assistant-side facts, with timestamps, speaker and provenance. Keep raw episodes too.
2. **Retrieval**: hybrid semantic + keyword + entity fusion, with temporal filters, then an answer-side "select relevant memories, then answer" step.
3. **Consolidation**: asynchronous "sleep-time" jobs (Letta, LightMem) that summarize, version beliefs and build entity profiles, without destroying source facts.
4. **Forgetting**: done through ranking, decay and invalidation, not hard deletes, except for user-requested erasure.
5. **Raw-history search**: an agentic file or grep search over raw history for long-horizon agent tasks.
6. **Evaluation**: evaluate on BEAM, LongMemEval-V2 or MemoryAgentBench plus an in-house test with a fixed judge, rather than quoting LoCoMo.

### Cited Findings
- **Sleep-time compute** (Letta, arXiv 2504.13171, April 2025): about 5× less test-time compute at matched accuracy on Stateful GSM-Symbolic and Stateful AIME, and up to +13% / +18% accuracy when scaling sleep-time compute. These are math benchmarks, not memory QA. Letta's sleep-time agents edit the primary agent's in-context memory blocks asynchronously. **[secondary for the numbers]** — [Latent Space wiki on Letta](https://latent.space/wiki/letta-ai-558a42a5/); [Letta agent memory blog](https://www.letta.com/blog/agent-memory)
- **Letta Context Repositories / MemFS** (March 2026): agent memory is a git-backed filesystem. Subagents reflect on slices of history in separate worktrees and merge results back into "main" memory, which is consolidation through versioned, mergeable files. **[self-reported, no eval found]** — [Letta context repositories](https://letta.com/blog/context-repositories); [Letta docs](https://docs.letta.com/letta-agent/memory/index.md)
- Letta's filesystem-based agent (gpt-4o-mini) scored 74.0 on LoCoMo, roughly equal to full-context gpt-4o-mini (74.0 reproduced). — [Mem0 2026 roundup](https://mem0.ai/blog/state-of-ai-agent-memory-2026); [braindetox](https://braindetox.kr/en/posts/memory_benchmark_scores_reliability_2026.html)
- **Agentic search over raw trajectories** (AgentRunbook-C) is the best system on LongMemEval-V2 (74.9% / 70.1%), and frontier LLMs without evidence score ≤14.1%. — [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/67423)
- **Graph memory trend**: Mem0 moved from an external graph store to built-in entity linking and removed relation traversal, arguing this is a net improvement for teams that don't want a separate graph database. Zep/Graphiti keeps a bi-temporal KG (episodes → entities/facts → communities, with validity intervals). New 2026 temporal-graph papers include SodaMem (evidence-grounded temporal graph memory, arXiv 2608.08055) and MESA (task-adaptive multi-structure evidence selection, arXiv 2608.10108); their numbers were not verified. — [Mem0 2026](https://mem0.ai/blog/state-of-ai-agent-memory-2026); [agentic-memory repo](https://github.com/lhl/agentic-memory); [SodaMem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08055); [MESA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10108)
- **Hierarchical / typed memory wins over flat**: Mem-α (core/episodic/semantic) far exceeds flat-paragraph MEM1 and MemAgent on MemoryAgentBench. Hindsight's four networks (facts / experiences / entity summaries / beliefs) reach 83.6% with a 20B open model vs 39% full-context. — [Mem-α](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25911v1); [Hindsight](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12818)
- **Compression before memory**: LightMem's LLMLingua-2 pre-compression and topic segmentation drive its 20–117× token savings, with equal or better accuracy than FullText in most configurations. — [LightMem](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18866v4)
- **Open problems** named in 2026 industry writing include temporal abstraction, cross-session structure, staleness, privacy and identity resolution. Procedural memory is described as "the least mature". — [Mem0 2026](https://mem0.ai/blog/state-of-ai-agent-memory-2026)
- **Surveys and 2026 framing**: "Always-On Agents" (arXiv 2606.30306) surveys persistent memory, state and governance. MemCog (arXiv 2605.28046) argues for "memory-as-cognition" over "memory-as-tool". Mi-Memory (arXiv 2607.18975) proposes a lifecycle memory framework. These are titles only and their contents were not verified. — [Always-On Agents](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.30306); [MemCog](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.28046); [Mi-Memory](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.18975)

### Inferences
- **Adopt:** append-only timestamped fact store plus raw episode archive; hybrid retrieval (vector + BM25 + entity); answer-time memory selection; async sleep-time consolidation into entity profiles and belief summaries with provenance links; bi-temporal validity (Graphiti-style invalidation) for facts that change.
- **Consider:** an agentic "search the raw logs" tool, either a filesystem or git-backed context repo, for agent-experience and procedural memory; LLMLingua-style compression to cut ingest cost; distilling a small extraction model from frontier traces once volume justifies it.
- **Defer:** RL-trained memory managers (unless you self-host models and have outcome signals); parametric and test-time-training memory.
- **Evaluate honestly:** fix the judge model and prompt, and report on LongMemEval-S (post-Sept-2025 file), BEAM at 1M and 10M, and LongMemEval-V2 if you are building agent memory. Always include a full-context baseline with the same answer model, plus a cost per query.

### Gaps
- No primary-source numbers were collected for MIRIX (arXiv 2507.07957), MemoryOS (arXiv 2506.06326), Nemori (arXiv 2508.03341), HippoRAG successors, or Graphiti 2026 changes. These need follow-up.
- No neutral cost and latency comparison (p50/p95, $ per 1K queries) across products exists in public sources found.
- Claims like "sleep-time designs in production at major labs" were unsourced and are excluded.
